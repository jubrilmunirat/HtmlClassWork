Dr. Norman Borlaug

The man who saved a billion lives

Dr. Norman Borlaug from the left, trains biologist in Mexico on how to increased wheat yeilds - part of his life-long war on hunger.

Here's a time line of Dr. Borlaug's life:

  • 1914 - Born in Cresco, Iowa
  • 1933 - Leaves his family's farm to attend the University of Minnesota,
    thanks to a Depression era program known as the "National Youth
    Administration"
  • 1935 - Has to stop school and save up more money. Works in Civillian
    Conservation Corps, helping starving Americans."I saw how food changed
    them," he said. "All of this left scars on me."
  • 1937 - Finishes University and takes a job in the US Forestry Service
  • 1938 - Marries wife of 69years Margaret Gibson. Get laid off due to budget
    cuts. Inspired by Elvin Charles Stakman, he returns to school to study under
    Stakman, who teaches him about breeding pests-resistant plants.
  • 1941 - Tries to enroll in the military after the Pearl Harbor attack, but is
    rejected. Instead, the military asked his lab to work on waterproof glue, DDT
    to control malaria, disinfectants and other appplied science.
  • 1942 - Receives a Ph.D. in Genetics and Plant Pathology
  • 1944 - Rejects a 100,000 dollar offer from DuPont, leave behind his
    pregnant wife, and flies to Mexico to head a new plant pathology program.
    Over the next 16 years, his teams breeds 6,000 different strains of dieases
    resistent wheat - including different vairities for each major climate on
    Earth.
  • 1945 - Discovers a way to grown wheat twice each season, doubling wheat
    yields.
    • 1953 - Crosses a short, study dwarf breed of wheat with a high-yeilding
    American breed, creating a strains hat responded well to fertilizer. It goes on
    to provide 95% of Mexico's wheat.
  • 1962 - Visits Delhi and brings its high - yeilding strains of wheat to the
    Indian subcontinent in time to help mitigate mass starvation due to a rapidly
    expanding population.
  • 1970 - receives Noble Peace Prize.
  • 1983 - helps seven African countries dramatically increase their maize and
    sorghum yields.
  • 1984 - Becomes a distinguished professor at Texas A&M University
  • 2005 - States "we will have to double the world food supply by 2050.
    " Argues that genetically modified crops are the only way we can meet the
    demand, as we run out of arable land. Says that GM crops are not inherently
    dangerous because "we've been genetically modifying plants and animals
    for a long time. Long before we called it science, people were selecting the
    best breeds."
  • 2009 - dies at the age of 95.

"Borlaug's life and achievement are testimony to the far-reaching
contributions that on man's towering intellect, persistence and scientific
vision can make to human peace and progress."

-- Indian Prime Minister Manmohan Singh
